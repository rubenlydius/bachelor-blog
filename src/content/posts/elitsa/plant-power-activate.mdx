After validating our concept, it was time to look at it through the eyes of our visitors. We were eager to see what an evening at Transfo Intiem looks like for them and where our installation might fit in. To find out, we used techniques we learned in our Experience Design classes.

## Why, how, what

We started with the golden circle. Instead of starting with what our installation is, you start with why it exists:

- **Why:** to give families and groups a calm, shared moment on a winter evening, and to show that breath connects all living things, while giving the old power station a new form of life by bringing nature inside.
- **How:** by letting visitors bring light to a sleeping wall, where roots grow from every lit spot, connect with the roots of others and slowly breathe.
- **What:** an interactive projection in the Pompenzaal.

## A night out with Sarah

Next, we brought back our AI persona, Sarah, a mother of two kids, Lou (5) and Tibo (9). Last year she went to Transfo Intiem and loved how calm and magical it felt, but one of her kids got bored at some stops, while the other couldn't keep up at others. We listed her goals and tasks for the evening and wrote a user scenario about her family's visit this year. It follows her path from arriving at the busy Pompenzaal to "growing" a flower with light together with her kids. We colour-coded the scenario, so you can see who she is, what she wants, the context, her path through the evening, and the problems she runs into.

<FigmaEmbed src={board} node="436-4326" title="Persona Sarah, goals, tasks and user scenario" caption="Our AI persona Sarah, her goals and tasks, and her user scenario." align="wide" />

## Drawing it out

To see what the experience could look like, we also drew a storyboard of a family visit in six frames. The family walks into the Pompenzaal and notices something moving on the dark wall, where a torch from the table is already shining. The youngest child runs to the table, where a small sign says "Grab one", takes a flashlight, and roots start to grow wherever she moves the light. Her brother takes one too and grows his own roots on the other wall. When their roots meet, they connect and flowers start to bloom. The wall "breathes", and they feel like they made it happen together.

<FigmaEmbed src={board} node="517-4022" title="Storyboard of a family visit" caption="Our storyboard of a family visit to the breathing wall." align="wide" />

## Mapping the journey

To go into more detail, we made a customer journey map of her evening in twelve steps, from planning the evening to going home, using the Devine template. For every step we wrote down what she does, how she feels, what she thinks, and which touchpoints she meets. Her mood goes up and down, marked by the emojis. She begins a bit doubtful at home, gets excited when they arrive, feels a bit overwhelmed when the Pompenzaal is full of people, and is happiest when the roots connect and a flower starts to bloom. The moment of truth is when she sees that her kids understand what to do and interact on their own: Lou grabs a torch and the roots react straight away. If that first reaction is slow, the magic is gone.

<FigmaEmbed src={board} node="478-4403" title="Customer journey map" caption="Sarah's customer journey with actions, feelings, thoughts, touchpoints, pain points and opportunities." align="wide" />

## From pain points to opportunities

For every pain point we wrote a "how might we" question. Some are about the evening in general, like keeping it calm at peak hours. Others are about our installation: how might we show what to do without any text, how might we make sure everyone in a group has their own light, and how might we keep the wall beautiful when lots of lights are on it. These questions remind us what matters most while designing.

## What we learned

Writing the scenario and the journey made our concept feel a lot more real. It also showed us that the moments around our installation matter too. By the time families reach the Pompenzaal, they are already tired, cold and a bit overwhelmed, so our installation has to work for them in that state.

## Next steps

Next, we will take the client's feedback on our pitch into account and write out our final concept. In the meantime, we start mapping how the installation works with the visitor flow, what the wall does with one light or many, and how everything is set up in the room. We also start our first tech tests.
