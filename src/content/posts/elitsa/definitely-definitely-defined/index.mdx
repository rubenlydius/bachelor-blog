Our concept started as one big wall where roots grow from the visitors' light. But the space kept changing, and so did the concept. In the end it became two scenes that tell one story.

## Three canvases on the back wall

When we still thought we would be on the back wall of the Pompenzaal, we noticed that the beams and seams in the concrete split the wall into three "canvases". That gave us an idea: what if we tell a story in three scenes? It would also solve a practical problem. Our laptops can't run more than two projectors at once, and we have no budget to rent MadMapper. With separate scenes, every laptop only needs to run its own projector.

<Figure src={backWall} alt="The back wall of the Pompenzaal, with three white rectangles drawn between the seams in the concrete" caption="The back wall, with room for three separate projections." align="wide" />

## Three scenes, one story

We tried out different ways to split the story over three scenes, roughly following the life of a plant:

- **Scene 1, seeds:** visitors bring water to seeds in the soil, so they start to grow.
- **Scene 2, stems:** stems grow from the light and take back the old factory.
- **Scene 3, flowers:** visitors wake up the flowers with their light, and they bloom and breathe.

But splitting the stems from the flowers broke the heart of our concept. In our original idea, flowers bloom where the stems of different people connect. That's the moment of togetherness. With the flowers on a separate wall, blooming had nothing to do with connecting anymore. So we went back to two scenes: one with seeds and water, and one with our original concept.

## A blessing in disguise

Then we heard that we had to move to another wall (see <a class="pill" href={withBase('/posts/room-for-change/')}>Room for change →</a>). It turned out to be a blessing in disguise. The left wall of the Pompenzaal is split by concrete beams into framed sections, which fit two scenes perfectly. No more squeezing three scenes onto one wall: the space now matched the story.

<Figure src={leftWall} alt="The left wall of the Pompenzaal, with white rectangles drawn between the concrete beams, a hook circled in pink and white lines showing how light strips could hang" caption="Left wall for the win: two naturally framed canvases." align="wide" />

## Scene 1: many, many versions

The second scene was clear, but the first one went through a lot of versions, including:

- Seeds in the soil, with a stream of water that follows your light.
- Water pipes you fill by moving your light through them. It looked fun, but it worked more like a puzzle and needed instructions.
- Leading seed sprouts to the top of the soil, so they could grow into plants.
- Branches that look like electricity, which turn into water when you shine on them. Transfo was a power station, so this felt like a nice transformation.
- Bulbs instead of seeds, once we found out that lilies grow from bulbs.

We made storyboards and visualised what came out of our brainstorms. Seeing everything in colour really helped us choose our direction.

<Figure src={scene1Ideas} alt="Frames of scene 1 ideas: roots on dark soil, water pipes in a maze, branches lighting up blue around glowing bulbs, a wave of water washing over branches, and seeds sprouting in brown soil" caption="Visualisations of some of the ideas for scene 1." align="wide" />

### Where we are at

In the end, we came up with a concept that keeps all the parts we liked, and tells a bit more about the new kind of breath on the Transfo site.

Sleeping bulbs lie between root branches that look like electricity, and they slowly breathe. Wherever visitors point their light, water follows. The branches fill with water and turn blue, and when the water reaches a bulb, it sprouts. When all bulbs have sprouted, the water rises and washes them like a wave toward scene 2. Then new bulbs appear in a new random layout. More lights wake the bulbs faster, so the whole group works toward one shared goal, and the wave invites everyone to move on to the next scene.

## Scene 2: growing together

The second scene is our original concept. Stems grow from wherever the light lands, and when the stems of different people meet, they connect, and lilies bloom and breathe. One torch always shines, so visitors can see what happens before they join, and it naturally shows them what to do.

## Updating the concept definition

With two scenes, our concept definition needed an update. We kept the first version on our board and made an updated one. The main changes:

- The story is now told in two scenes, from sleeping bulbs to blooming lilies.
- The camera films the visitors instead of the wall, because our tracking tests showed that works much better (see <a class="pill" href={withBase('/posts/pretty-spot-on/')}>Pretty spot on →</a>).
- The interactions table is split over the two scenes.
- "How it works", the materials and the budget match our new setup, with one projector, laptop and camera per scene.
- LED strips on the beams look like root branches and add to the atmosphere.

We also did more research for scene 1: lilies grow from bulbs (RHS, n.d.), water makes people feel calmer (White et al., 2010), people try harder when they see their progress (Kivetz et al., 2006), and movement draws the eye (Abrams & Christ, 2003), which is why the wave pulls people on to scene 2.

## More layers of meaning

The new scene also added new connections to our meaning map. A bulb is both a lily bulb and a light bulb, which needs power, like the power the station once made. The Pompenzaal once pumped water, and now water wakes new life. Together, they tell the story of the site: power turns into water, and water into life.

<FigmaEmbed src={board} node="1486-24488" title="Our meaning map with new connections" caption="Our meaning map with new connections." align="wide" />

## What we learned

Changing the space forced us to rethink the concept, and that made it stronger. Splitting the story into scenes only works if every scene keeps what makes our concept special, which is why we chose two scenes instead of three. And iterating a lot on scene 1 helped us find an idea that needs no instructions, fits the site and still brings people together.
