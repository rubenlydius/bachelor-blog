The brief says the installation is for everyone from 5 to 80 years old. That is almost everyone, and you can't really design for "everyone". So before we started on ideas, we wanted a clearer picture of who actually walks into the Pompenzaal on that evening.

## Five boxes, two versions

We used the five categories from Experience Design: demographics, geography, technology, behaviour and psychographics. In the first version we wrote down everything we could think of. It was a lot, and some of it was more guessing than knowing. In the second version we cleaned it up and kept what really matters for an evening like this. Most people come as a family or a group, they walk the route at their own pace and only stop for a minute or two. Kids run ahead, while adults follow, watch and film. The things we were not sure about, we marked so we can check them later, like whether people even want to use their own phone at an event like this.

From the five boxes we wrote one user group sentence:

<Breakout>Families and mixed-age groups (5-80) from Zwevegem and the Kortrijk region who visit Transfo Intiem on a cold December evening to spend time together.</Breakout>

**Traits:** they walk the route at their own pace, stop for a minute or two at each installation and need something a 5 year old gets straight away and an 80 year old still finds beautiful.

## The nerd and the noob, but younger and older

In our earlier projects we used the nerd and the noob as our extremes. Here the biggest difference between visitors is age, so we picked a 5 year old and an 80 year old. The 5 year old runs ahead, touches everything first and needs a reaction within seconds, or they move on. The 80 year old comes with the grandchildren, stands back and won't touch anything unless it clearly invites them. For both of them we wrote down what we need to design for: instant, physical feedback at child height for the youngest, and something that is beautiful to watch, with room to stand aside, for the oldest. If both of them enjoy the installation, everyone in between will too.

## Who else is in the room?

The visitors are not the only people we have to think about. With an ecosystem map we put the visitors in the middle and drew everyone around them. Close by are the other visitors, the performers and the other student teams in the Pompenzaal. Around them are the site, the technical crew and the vendors, and on the outside the organisers and our school. This helped us stay in our own corner, literally: it reminded us that our installation shares a room and a route with a lot of other people, so it can't be loud or take over the whole space.

## Meet Sarah

To keep a real person in mind, we made an AI persona. Sarah is 38, a primary school teacher from Zwevegem and a mother of two kids, Lou (5) and Tibo (9). She went to Transfo Intiem last year and liked how calm it felt, but she noticed that Tibo got bored at some stops while Lou couldn't keep up at others. Her quote sums up what we are aiming for:

<Breakout variant="plain">"If the kids are excited and I get a nice moment too, the evening was worth it."</Breakout>

Sarah's details are still made up, based on the brief and what we knew at this point. We want to check them with real people from our target audience later on.

## What we learned

Defining the target audience made the brief a lot less vague. "Everyone from 5 to 80" turned into families on a short stop, with kids who lead and adults who watch. A lot of what we wrote down is still an assumption, though. That is why we're ready to change it once we hear from real visitors, which is one of our next to-dos.

## Next steps

Now we knew who we were designing for, but we still needed to know more about the place, the event and the theme itself. Next up: our research.
