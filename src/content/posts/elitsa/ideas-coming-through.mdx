With our research summed up in insight cards, we were finally allowed to think about ideas. Before we started, though, we wanted something to judge those ideas with, something that made sure the ideas we choose follow the requirements.

## Rules before ideas

From the insight cards we wrote five design principles: No instructions needed, Experienced together, Felt, not only seen, Calm in a busy hall and Beautiful to watch too. Every principle comes from one or more insights. For example, "Experienced together" comes from the fact that people visit as families and groups, and that a queue makes a group split up. We made the principles before brainstorming on purpose. That way we could check every idea against them later, instead of picking the one we personally liked best.

## Brain dump

Our very first ideas actually came earlier, right after reading the brief. We made a brain map around the word "breath" and split it into a literal side, like inhale and exhale, visible breath in the cold and fogged glass, and an abstract side, like slowing down, being present and breathing new life into something. Everyone then wrote down first ideas on sticky notes, and we collected references we liked. Ideas about nature, plants and photosynthesis already showed up here.

## A kite, of all things

For a proper brainstorm, we started from our refined HMW and used random word association. Our random word was "kite". From kite we went to wind, string, sky, colours, children, paper and falling, and turned each word into an idea about breath. Wind became a projected dandelion you blow on, and the seeds grow where they land. String became lines between visitors' light spots that grow into a web of roots. Children became "the smallest lights grow the biggest plants". We also added our own ideas along the way, like curtains with air blowing through them, and a projection that starts in black and white and gets colour the more people join.

## Flipping it around

Our bias technique was flipped assumptions. We wrote down what we assumed about breath installations and turned it upside down. "Breath is invisible" became "breath is the most visible thing in the room". "You have to do something to interact" became "doing nothing is the interaction", which led to roots that only grow when you stand still and breathe slowly. It was a good way to get away from the obvious ideas.

## Spot the pattern

When we put all the ideas together in one list, we noticed a pattern. A lot of them were about light, plants and nature: plants that grow toward light, a garden that wakes up, a factory that nature takes back. We marked those with a heart. They became the ingredients for our chili (sin carne converging method).

<FigmaEmbed src={board} node="90-6778" title="All ideas in one list" caption="All our ideas in one list. The hearts mark the ideas about light, plants and nature." align="wide" />

## What we learned

Making the design principles first made brainstorming easier, because we knew what an idea had to do. The techniques helped us get past our first ideas. Still, the strongest theme, light and plants, was there from the very start. The brainstorm mostly helped us see it more clearly.

## Next steps

With a list full of hearts, it was time to mix the best ideas into one concept. But first, we visited Transfo. Next up: getting to know Transfo from up close.
