Our bachelor project started with a brief from Transfo Zwevegem for Transfo Intiem 2026. This is a free annual event that invites people to enjoy an immersive winter evening walk on the old Transfo power station site. This year's theme is "Breath" and the event will be held on 12 December. The brief gives us two options: an interactive installation in the Pompenzaal or a visual installation for the soundscapes of the Woord students from the Kunstacademie. We didn't know yet which one we would do, so we tried to keep our ideas open enough to work for both.

## Getting everything in one place

Before doing anything else, we put the whole project on one board: the brief, the learning outcomes and when they are assessed, every deadline, our team roles and all the info about the site and the event. We created our own systems, since having everything on the FigJam makes it very easy to check something later on.

<FigmaEmbed src={board} node="285-3236" title="FigJam board with the brief, learning outcomes and deadlines" align="wide" />

While going through the brief we were writing down different things we noticed and were important to the assignment, but we also had many questions.

## Questioning ourselves or the brief

<Breakout variant="plain">"Can we choose the assignment? Is there a budget for projectors and sensors? Is the Pompenzaal heated, and is it dark? How long should the experience be?…"</Breakout>

We wrote all of them down, so that we could ask them at a later date. We split them into questions for our coach, about the assignment and the pitch, and questions for the client, about budget, materials and the space itself. We got answers fairly quickly. Our coach told us the Friday pitch was a feedback session with the other Transfo teams and that a first concept or insight was enough. The client planned a meeting with us to visit the Transfo site on Thursday, 29 October, which would be a great opportunity for us as we can ask and discuss everything on our minds and in our notes, all while touring the actual event site and getting inspiration.

<FigmaEmbed src={board} node="64-3418" title="FigJam section with feedback and notes from the coach and client" align="left" ratio="4 / 5" />

We added a section in our FigJam where we could write all the feedback we received plus the conversations we had with the coach and client. This way we could refer to them at any time. We also could use the notes to improve our concept later on.

## To dos and to don'ts

We also made a to-do list per discipline (general, UX, design and development), a weekly planning until the jury in December, a schedule with all deadlines and the client's dates, and a hand-in checklist. The first deadline was quite close: a first concept pitch with the other students working with Transfo on Friday, 30 October, and the pitch deck with a video explanation for the client on Sunday, 1 November.

<FigmaEmbed src={board} node="285-3238" title="FigJam section with the to-do lists, planning and deadlines" align="wide" />

## What we learned

The brief is very open, and we had more questions than answers. Writing everything down early helped us see what we didn't know yet, and that became the starting point for our problem space definition.

Looking back, the first week went really fast. Some things, like talking to people from our target audience, had to move to the next weeks.

## Next steps

With a board full of open questions, it was time to understand the problem itself. Next up: our problem space definition.
