The Pompenzaal is a big, beautiful hall, but we don't have it to ourselves. Where our installation goes decides almost everything: which wall we project on, how many projectors we need, how visitors walk past and even how our concept works. In just a few weeks, our place in the hall changed three times.

## Three teams, one hall

At first, three teams would share the Pompenzaal. We drew different layouts of where each team could go, and two walls stood out for us: the back wall opposite the entrance, and the wall between the entrance and the exit. To choose between them, we first had to know how big we could actually project.

### How big can we project?

We looked at the projectors the client could lend us, and ranked them from strongest to weakest. It wasn't sure yet which ones would be available for us:

- 4× Epson EB-L260F
- 3× Optoma EH460ST (short throw)
- 1× projector of 4000 lumen, 1× of 3200 lumen and 3× of 3000 lumen

The wall is about 12.8 m wide. One short-throw projector can project about 4.2 × 2.4 m (16:9) without losing much quality, so three projectors next to each other cover about 12.6 × 2.4 m: almost the whole wall.

## The first plan

After the concept pitches, we met with the other teams to divide the hall. We discussed a lot about the visitor flow, connecting our concepts and creating somewhat of a story for the visitor to experience. We agreed that we would be the last team in the hall, projecting on the wall between the entrance and the exit (possibly also on a white curtain on the side, but that idea quickly got shut down when we actually tried tracking light on a curtain).

</TwoColumn>

<TwoColumn src={twoTeams} alt="Floor plan of the Pompenzaal with our space at the back wall, marked with green plant scribbles, and the other team's space on the right, marked with pink lines, a question mark and an arrow" caption="Layout with two teams sharing the Pompenzaal." wide top>

## Two teams, a lot of space

A few days later, one of the teams (the circle in our sketch) moved to another room. That left the whole Pompenzaal to split between two teams. We tried different layouts and chose this one: a corridor from the entrance to the back wall, where our projection would be, and from there the other team's corridor towards the exit, since they wanted visitors to walk through a corridor. We met with the other team, and we all agreed on it.

</TwoColumn>

## Back to the drawing board

The next day, the other team visited the Pompenzaal for the first time, and together with the client they decided that their concept fits best on the back wall. So we were back to the drawing board.

We looked again at the wall between the entrance and the exit, and also at the wall to the left of the entrance. That wall has windows, but they're high up, so there's still enough room for our projection. Concrete beams split it into separate sections. At first that seemed like a problem, but we had already been thinking about separate screens instead of one big one, since our laptops can't run more than two projectors at once, and we have little to no budget to rent MadMapper. The beams turned that into a natural choice: each section becomes its own scene. We also really liked the look of the wall, and the beams have holes where we could mount our LED strips.

<Figure src={beams} alt="Photo of the wall to the left of the entrance, with white rectangles between the concrete beams, a hook circled in pink and white lines showing how light strips could hang from it" caption="What we really liked: the beams acting like a frame, and the holes would be perfect for light strips." align="wide" />

When we met with the other team again, we agreed to their placement, and we took the wall to the left of the entrance.

</TwoColumn>

## What we learned

The space decides much more than we expected: the number of projectors, the number of scenes, and even the concept. It helped that our concept is flexible, because roots can grow on any wall, and splitting it into scenes didn't break the idea: it let us go deeper. We also learned to look at the real space before agreeing on a plan, and to keep talking with the other team, because every change for them was a change for us too.
