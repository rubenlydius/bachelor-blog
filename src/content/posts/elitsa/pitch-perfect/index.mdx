## What do we present?

For the concept pitch we got a list of criteria: get to know each other's projects, the pros and cons if you'd sell it to your client, where you are, the feedback from your client, something unique, the next steps, and issues you want to bring to the group. It didn't say how to present, which makes sense, since every team has a different client. So we asked our coach. The answer: we could choose how to present our concept, as long as we covered the criteria.

## Answering the criteria first

Before making any slides, we wrote down our answer to each criterion. That made it much easier to decide what to show:

- **Pros:** it doesn't need a big budget, it fits the theme of breath and the site's history, it works for all ages, and there's almost nothing to learn.
- **Cons:** it needs a dark space for the tracking, the tracking can make mistakes, and many bright flashlights at once can make the projection less clear.
- **Where we are:** testing our coded demos, creating the visuals and doing coding experiments.
- **Client feedback:** they liked the concept, and asked us to keep the budget in mind.
- **Unique:** the flashlight tracking. No app and almost no instructions, just a torch. It works for all ages, alone or in a group, and it has a deeper meaning.
- **Next steps:** more testing rounds to find the pitfalls, finalising the visuals and the code, and figuring out the LED strips.

## Our question for the group

For the issues part, we asked about the promotional text for the festival, because that deadline was coming up and we weren't sure what was expected. The advice was that it's probably a caption and maybe a short paragraph, but to ask the client directly. So we did.

The client told us they need a few sentences we can use as a caption for a social media post, and a promotional visual to go with it. We could look at the posts already on the Transfo Intiem Facebook page or the website as an example. One thing to keep in mind was that the image shouldn't give away the whole installation yet.

Most of those posts are more like a short bio than a caption. For us, a few sentences that make people curious, and a visual that only shows a hint of the wall, should be enough.

## Making the breathing visible

We also told the group that the breathing doesn't come across yet. Our coach suggested putting something small in front of the installation that explains it, and someone from another team agreed. But in all our testing rounds, people said they'd rather experience it first and read about it after. When we shared that, they agreed, and our coach said we should go with our test results.

So the plan is now to make a poster at the start with the name, *Breathe back to light*, and an image, and after the installation a poster with a short explanation.

## Other things we took from it

- Our school will ask the client to cover insurance for our laptops. It won't cost much, but we will keep it in mind for the budget.
- If the client doesn't answer, it's fine to send another message (not too often!).
- Other teams send their client pictures of their progress now and then. Clients mostly care that it works, not about the concept, so a short update with pictures is enough. We will make sure to send them one as soon as possible.

## Next steps

- Write the caption and make a visual that doesn't give everything away.
- Design the posters: one for promotion, and two for the installation: a title poster at the start and an explanation after the installation.
- Send the client small updates with pictures.
- Keep testing and finalise the visuals and the code.
