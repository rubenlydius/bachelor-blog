## What we changed after round 1

Round 1 gave us a clear to-change list (see <a class="pill" href={withBase('/posts/spend-once-test-twice/')}>Spend once, test twice →</a>), so before round 2 we worked through most of it:

- Less blur and less movement, so the flowers don't look like they vanish
- Leaves on the stems (but not animated, yet?)
- Roots that stay longer: move fast and the trail is short, move slowly and it gets longer
- Flowers that appear reliably where roots connect, so it's easier to understand
- Buttercups for normal growth, so the lilies stand out as the flower you get from connecting
- A first, simple version of scene 1
- A clearer picture sign

Still open from round 1: making the breathing clearer, a short text to read after the installation, and sound.

## A clearer sign

In round 1, the sign was just a few pictures without any words, and one tester had to look at it a few times to understand what to do. For round 2 we made a clearer version. It worked much better: the group knew right away to use their flashlight.

## Round 2: one group of three

We tested a simple version of scene 1 together with the updated scene 2. We didn't have 2 cameras or 2 projectors, so we switched between the scenes ourselves. This time we tested with a group of three at once. At the festival, people come with friends and share the wall with strangers, so we wanted to see how they figure it out together.

We added tasks for scene 1, and a few extra questions: what should happen when all the bulbs have sprouted, when they would stop playing and walk on, whether they would take a torch or use their phone, and more.

## What we found

- The meaning of scene 1 came across without any context. The group described it as "the essentials of life" and "breathing life into something".
- Scene 2 was the most fun. They played, drew and wrote with their lights, and found out by themselves that connecting makes a flower, and that moving slower makes the lines last longer.
- The bulbs looked like dots or molecules, not seeds. They need the 3D models.
- Scene 1 has no clear end, because the bulbs shrink back when the light moves away.
- Nobody saw the breathing on their own. The flowers disappear too fast to see them pulse.
- They want to read about the meaning at the end, not on the projection.
- One tester suggested more electricity, like a plasma ball, with the current running from seed to seed.

## Round 1 vs round 2

What got better:

- The meaning came across much better. In round 1, people mostly said "flowers grow from light". Now they talked about the essentials of life, without us explaining anything.
- The connection is clear: they found out how flowers appear on their own.
- The sign worked: everyone started right away.
- People still drew and wrote, but on their own, not because of our task.

What didn't change yet:

- The breathing still doesn't come across.
- People still prefer to read about the meaning after playing. From our pitch, some would also like just a little something at the beginning (see <a class="pill" href={withBase('/posts/pitch-perfect/')}>Pitch perfect →</a>).
- There is still no sound.

And something else: with scene 1 added, we might have to discuss whether it needs a clear ending or can stay open. Both have their advantages and disadvantages. Without a clearer ending, people might spend too much time on it. But if it is more structured, it might remove the exploratory side of it and the freedom that the testers have enjoyed until now.

## Plan for round 3

- Test the new version with about 5 people: one group and a couple of people on their own
- Use both projections at once, like it will be in the hall
- Bring a real basket of torches and see what people pick
- Keep the core tasks the same, so we can compare the rounds
- Check our fixes: do the bulbs look like seeds, does scene 1 feel finished, and do people see the breathing without help?

## Next steps

Next, we apply the feedback, finish scene 1, and keep working on both scenes in general: the styling and all the small details.

At the same time, we begin working on the promotional text and visuals, and the signs that will go in the installation hall. Next up: Sign the design!
