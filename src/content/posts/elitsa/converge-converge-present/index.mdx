After the brainstorm and our visit to Transfo, we had a long list of ideas, a lot of new info about the Pompenzaal, and very little time until the first pitch. Time to bring everything together.

## Cooking up a concept

To converge we used the Chilli sin Carne method. The idea is simple and fun: you take the best parts of different ideas and mix them into one "dish". We took the ideas with a heart, like light-flowers on the ceiling, shadows that grow roots, a photosynthesis visualisation and interacting with a flashlight, and looked at what they had in common. Then we split them into basics, the things the concept can't work without, and extras, the things that would be nice if we have time. From that mix we wrote a first, rough definition of the concept.

## What does it all mean?

We also made a meaning map, to see which layers we were combining. In the middle is "Breathe back to light" (bring back to life), and around it are photosynthesis, our own breath, the site's past, light in the dark winter and being together. Plants need light to breathe, and they give back the oxygen we breathe in. The power station once burned coal and heated up the canal, and now visitors give the place back to nature. Kids don't need to know any of that to enjoy it, but for adults it adds a second, more meaningful layer.

## Breathe back to light

Then we wrote the concept out in three levels: a really rough version, a full description and a short version for the pitch. In short: a wall in the Pompenzaal is covered in nature elements that slowly pulse, as if they are breathing. Visitors bring light, with their own phone flashlight or a torch from the table, and wherever the light falls, roots (fractal patterns) start to grow. With one light the wall stays quite empty, with only a few leaves. The more people join, the more the roots connect, leaves appear and flowers begin to bloom. One torch on the table already points at the wall, so the wall is already "alive" as an example, and people know what to do.

## But can we build it?

We also had to think about whether we could actually make this. We measured one of the walls as a possible projection surface, around 12.8 metres wide, and looked at which beamers the municipality has, including short throw beamers, so visitors don't cast shadows on the wall. In the team we had a misunderstanding about the camera. Two of us thought it would film the wall and see where the light lands, while the other thought it would hang on the wall and film the visitors. We went with filming the wall, because the roots should grow where the light falls. Whether the camera can tell torch light apart from the projection is the first thing we will test.

## Slides, styles and a pitch

For the pitch we turned our process into slides. We collected visual references, tried three styles and went for a dark one with glowing roots and small tracking boxes (blob tracking), because it felt the most like the installation itself.

<FigmaEmbed src={board} node="444-4063" title="Visual references and styles" caption="Visual references for the installation and the pitch." align="wide" ratio="16 / 8" />

Since the video for the client had to be less than 2 minutes, we had to keep it really short. On Friday we pitched to the other Transfo teams and gave them feedback, and the reactions to our concept were positive. We also used the session to start dividing the Pompenzaal between the teams.

## What we learned

Converging was harder than diverging. A lot of ideas were nice, but the Chilli sin Carne method and our design principles made it easier to choose. Thinking about the tech early also helped, because it showed us what we need to test first, but also what might not even be possible to create.

## Next steps

We sent our pitch deck and video to the client and are waiting for their feedback. Next up: writing the blog posts and developing the website.
