Before building our concept, we wanted to know whether it really holds up. "Did we miss anything?" and "What could possibly go wrong?" were our main questions for this step. To find out, we validated the concept with three techniques: six thinking hats, PPCO and MoSCoW. Each one looks at the concept from a different angle, which helped us explore what is crucial for the execution and how to make it even better.

## Wearing six different hats

We looked at the concept from six different perspectives: facts, feelings, cautions, benefits, creativity and process. The facts and feelings showed us that the concept fits a short, busy route and can feel calm and magical at the same time. The black hat was the most useful. It showed us that the light tracking is still untested, that the wall could get chaotic at peak hours, that the curtain could bend the projection and that torches could break or disappear. With the green hat, we tried to come up with solutions for the black hat problems, and with the blue hat we planned what needs to be done.

## Pluses, potentials and concerns

With PPCO we listed the pluses and potentials of the concept, and turned our concerns into "how might we" questions, each with a way to overcome it. This helped us see that most concerns are practical, not about the idea itself: reliable tracking, a busy wall, taking turns at peak hours and making the link to breath clear without text.

<FigmaEmbed src={board} node="239-3229" title="PPCO" caption="PPCO: pluses, potentials, concerns and how we could overcome them." align="wide" />

## Must, should, could, won't (MoSCoW)

With MoSCoW we sorted every feature by priority. The must-haves are the core: light tracking, roots that grow from the light and connect, and a table with torches that keeps the wall alive. Breathing, flowers and the curtain projection are should-haves, while extras like oxygen bubbles or 3D flowers are could-haves for when there's time. We also decided what we won't do, like requiring phones or adding written instructions. This keeps our focus on what really matters.

<FigmaEmbed src={board} node="242-3463" title="MoSCoW" caption="MoSCoW: what our concept must, should, could and won't have." align="wide" />

## What we learned

Looking at the concept from different angles showed us that its core works well. Most of our concerns turned out to be practical: reliable tracking, a busy wall at peak hours and making the link to breath clear without text. MoSCoW helped us decide what really matters for the event, and what can wait until we have time left.

## Next steps

Next, we wanted to find out what our concept actually relies on. So we wrote down our assumptions and looked for research that could support them or prove them wrong.
