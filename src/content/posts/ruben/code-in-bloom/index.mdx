The tracking tells us where every light is (see <a class="pill" href={withBase('/posts/pretty-spot-on/')}>Pretty spot on →</a>). Now something has to happen there. Our wall has two scenes: one where water wakes up a sleeping root network, and one where lights meet and a lily blooms between them. Here's how both were built.

## Overall structure

Every scene started as a small experiment: a separate web page (JavaScript/TypeScript + Vite) with a panel of sliders to tune it live.

- **The mouse first:** holding the mouse button counts as a flashlight, so I could test at my desk. The visuals only read a list of lights, so the tracking could later replace the mouse without changing anything else.
- **The right tool per layer:** the roots are drawn on a 2D canvas, the water runs on the GPU (WebGPU), and the flowers are 3D models in Three.js. Each layer has its own canvas, stacked on top of each other.
- **Versions, not rewrites:** each new version was a copy with one thing added, so I could always compare.

## Scene 1: bulbs, roots and water

### Growing toward the light

The first experiment had roots that grow toward light. Thousands of tiny agents, modelled on slime mould (physarum), follow each other's trails and form veins. It looked alive, but it was too creepy for a calm installation with the theme "breath".

### Adding water

The second version added water. It rests in pools, and when you press and drag, a stream of particles follows your path and falls back when you let go. The particles feel pressure, tension and gravity, and water that touches a root runs along it.

### A root network to wake up

The generated roots never looked like ours, so for `r-newscreen1` we started from a drawing. A small script traces it into branches around one central hub, and each branch sways slowly, as if breathing.

</TwoColumn>
- **Dim by default:** the roots sit at 5% opacity on black, light up where the water passes, then slowly fade back.
- **Water from the light:** while a light is on, water comes straight out of it, follows its path, and dries up after it lets go.
- **Bulbs:** when the water touches a bulb, small roots grow out of its bottom and a sprout grows up from its top. This is the start of the lily on the other screen.

## Scene 2: stems, connecting and lilies blooming

### The logic, without styling

I built the bare logic with plain circles first. Every light has an outer and an inner circle. When the outer circles touch, a line grows between the lights. When the inner circles touch, they're connected, and they stay connected until the outer circles separate again, so the connection doesn't flicker.

### Roots, trails and a flower

Next, every light drew a root trail with leaves: short when moving fast, long when moving slowly. Connections now happen between trails: two roots grow toward each other and a 3D lily grows where they meet. The first lily had a stem, which looked like a stick from above, so it went. Now the flower opens right on the meeting point.

### Holding still

A light that holds still drew nothing, which felt dead. Now three twigs sprout from it, at least 60° apart, taking turns to pull in and grow out elsewhere. Their tips also count as connection points, so a moving light can still connect to someone standing still.

## Bringing it together

`r-testing2` combines both scenes with the flashlight tracking, with a switch to swap between them. Every flashlight gets its own trail in scene 2 and its own water in scene 1, so we can easily simulate the installation for user testing.

## Next steps

Next, we test both scenes on a projection with users: how people interact, what they think about it, and whether everything runs smoothly with many lights at once. We're also making a 3D version of the room to get a better feeling of how the actual installation will be.
