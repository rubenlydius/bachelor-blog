## Looking for a new direction

After our first visual tryouts, we weren't fully sure about the style we had. It worked, but we felt it was lacking something. We started looking for new inspiration, and we found a style we really liked. It combined soft blurry effects, long-exposure movement and a frosted glass effect.

We each started gathering inspiration images to get a better idea of the possibilities, and also to see what each of us liked. We found a lot of different inspiration.

## Why this style?

We really liked this new style because it feels soft, a little magical, and fits our concept really well. When we tried to figure out exactly why it works so well, we came up with a few reasons:

- **Breath is something you feel more than see.** You can't really see someone breathing, but you can see its effects, like fog on a cold window or a soft haze in the air. The blur gives a similar feeling: something subtle that is there, but not completely visible.
- **It creates a dreamy feeling.** The soft and blurry images remind us a bit of memories or dreams. They make you want to stop and look closer, instead of immediately grabbing your attention. This fits the calm atmosphere we want to create in the busy hall.
- **It shows movement in a different way.** With the long-exposure effect, you don't just see the flower itself, but also a trace of its movement. When the petals open and close, it almost looks like the flower is breathing. They breathe slowly, at around 6 breaths a minute, because slow breathing like that is linked to a calmer body (Laborde et al., 2022).
- **It makes the digital elements feel more natural.** Sharp 3D graphics can sometimes look a bit like a computer game. By adding blur and transparency, the flowers feel more like photographs or paintings. It also makes them feel softer and more fragile. Blur also makes images less intense. In a study by De Cesarei and Codispoti (2010), blurred pictures caused a smaller stress reaction in the body, but people still felt the emotion, as long as they could recognise what they saw. So we never blur so much that a flower stops looking like a flower. We also keep the shapes soft and rounded, because people prefer curved objects over objects with sharp angles (Bar & Neta, 2006).
- **Not everything is blurred.** Like flowers behind frosted glass, the parts closest to the glass stay sharp and the rest fades. Sharp parts stand out straight away: people find a sharp object between blurred ones in only 200 ms (Kosara et al., 2002). We use this to guide the eye, so the spot where a visitor's light shines always stays sharp.
- **It works well with our installation.** Since we are projecting light onto a wall, it makes sense to use a soft visual style. Light naturally spreads and creates softer edges, so the style works with the projection instead of against it. It can also help hide some of the imperfections of projecting onto a concrete wall.
