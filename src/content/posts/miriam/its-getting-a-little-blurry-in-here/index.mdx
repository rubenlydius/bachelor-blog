## First tryouts on our lily

After we created our new styleboard, it was time to bring it to life for our concept. I started experimenting in code, by adding new visual effects on top of our 3D lily model.

The first effect I added was motion blur on the breathing. In our styleboard, we wanted the visuals to feel soft and dreamy, as if the movement leaves a trace behind it, like in long exposure photography. So while the lily slowly opens and closes, it leaves soft trails of itself behind, which makes the breathing feel calmer and more alive.

To find the right look, I added sliders for all the values:

- **Breathing:** how long the flower breathes in and out, how far it opens and closes, and how much it turns and sways while moving.
- **Long exposure:** how long the trails stay visible, how many copies of the flower they are made of, how soft they are and how much they smear along the movement.

## Particles during breathing

In the next tryout, I added particles on top of the breathing movement. As the flower slowly opens and closes, small particles scatter from its petals and gradually fade away. I played with how many particles there are, how big they are, how far they spread and how long they stay. They take their colour from the petals, get a soft blur and also show up in the long exposure trails.

To make them blend better with the flower, I also made the petal edges dissolve slightly and gave the petals a soft watercolour look.

## Oxygen particles

We also explored other ways to visualise the breathing and came up with the idea of oxygen particles rising from the centre of the flower. I experimented with small particles slowly floating upwards from the middle of the lily, like oxygen being released. I adjusted their quantity, rising speed, height, sideways movement, colour and blur to get the movement and appearance just right.

## See-through petals

Another thing I experimented with was making the petals see-through, to match the soft, dreamy look of our styleboard. I played with the opacity of the whole petal, the middle and the edges, and how softly they blend into each other. In the end, I went for a lower opacity in the middle of the petal and a higher opacity towards the edges, smoothly blended together.

## Combining roots and the new lily

Next, I combined the new lily with the earlier roots I built. I made the roots 3D so they look more realistic. Then I added the same blur on top of them, but not everywhere at once: some parts stay sharp while others are soft, and the blur slowly moves along the branches.

The new lilies now grow along the roots: first the stem and leaves appear, then the flower shows up closed and starts to open. After a while, the roots dissolve into small particles that fade away.

I also made all flowers open and close at the same time, so the whole wall breathes in sync and feels like one living thing instead of separate flowers.

## Working on the root logic

To make sure the roots always look good, I adjusted the rules for how they can grow:

- I played around with how many branches can grow.
- Branches can't grow in parallel next to each other.
- I adjusted how far a branch can grow away from your cursor while still following its movement. If a branch is too far away or would have to make a big turn, it stops, and a new branch starts from where your light is.
- When your light moves over an existing branch, no new branch grows. Instead, that branch becomes slightly thicker and a few leaves appear on it.

## Flower variety

To make the wall feel less repetitive, I added more variety to the flowers. Some grow on a long stem, some on a short one, and some grow directly on the branch, showing only the flower head. They also face different directions, from a side view to a top view. Fully open flowers only keep a subtle blur, so the wall doesn't feel too blurry.
