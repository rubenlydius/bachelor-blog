Once we knew what our installation should do (visitors shine a torch on the wall, roots grow from the light, roots connect and flowers bloom), the next big question was what it should look like. Our concept is about nature, growth and breath, but "nature" can look like a hundred different things. Before choosing, we wanted to see the options side by side.

## Ten different styleboards

I made ten styleboards, each one a different way of showing nature and growth:

1. **X-ray flowers:** white, see-through flowers on black.
2. **Ethereal black and white:** smoke, neurons and roots, glowing white on black.
3. **Generative and data-driven:** leaf veins, branching diagrams and growth patterns that look calculated.
4. **Cyanotype:** plants printed in white on deep blue, like old sun prints.
5. **Digital collage:** flowers on black, mixed with glitch effects and data overlays.
6. **Bioluminescent:** glowing blue and cyan plants, close to fantasy.
7. **Art Nouveau:** ornamental flowers and patterns, inspired by Mucha and Morris.
8. **Soft photography and line work:** blurry green plant photos, fractal trees and fine line drawings.
9. **Botanical illustration:** herbarium-style drawings in green and beige.
10. **Risograph posters:** bold blue graphic prints with plants.

The goal was not to pick one board. It was to find out what each of us likes in these directions, and to create a new styleboard based on that.

## Collecting feedback

My teammates went through the styleboards separately and left their feedback directly on the FigJam board. They marked what they liked, wrote short notes, and added extra references they had found themselves. After that, I went through their feedback and added my own opinion next to it.

<FigmaEmbed src={board} node="854-6864" title="Feedback on the styleboards" caption="Our FigJam board with the feedback and notes we left about the visual style." align="wide" />

**Realistic nature:** my teammates preferred realistic natural elements. Flat or stylised versions felt less magical to them. One suggestion was to keep the plants and flowers realistic, and add abstract or procedural elements on top of them, for example while the flowers are being animated.

**Colour palette:** a dark background, possibly with subtle gradients. The colour palette could start dark, with mostly green and white, and colour could come in when the flowers bloom. One teammate really likes green and pink, so we are considering these as our main colours.

**Lilies:** the realistic lily references stood out for the team. I really like them too, and I think we could use realistic lilies as the base and add an animation effect on top. In one reference, small dots of light travel towards the flower. A teammate suggested something similar for light travelling along the roots, or gathering where the torches shine.

**Social media inspiration:** we also collected quite a lot of inspiration from social media, from similar interactive experiments to possible visual directions.

## Main insights

The feedback points in a clear direction, even though it isn't final yet:

- realistic natural elements, with lilies as the main flower
- a more abstract, digital layer on top: the roots, the light and small particles that react to the torches
- a dark background, close to black
- little colour at the start, and colour when the flowers bloom
- no code or binary elements

The visual direction is much clearer now than when we started. We know we want realistic nature, lilies as the main flower, a dark background and colour that comes in when the flowers bloom. But there is still a lot to explore, for example how abstract the digital layer on top should be, how the roots grow and how the light moves. I think the best next step is to start doing small visual experiments to see what is possible and what works well.

## Next steps

Since realistic lilies came out as our main flower, I started with a first experiment: building a simple, low-poly lily in Blender that we can shape, animate and test in real time.
