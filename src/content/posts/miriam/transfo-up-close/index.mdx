As we started reading the brief for our bachelor project and talking about it with our team, several questions popped up that we needed answers to before we could start brainstorming ideas. We got in touch with the team at Transfo Zwevegem and agreed on a visit to the site itself.

## The questions we prepared

We arrived with a list:

- How big is the room?
- Will the room be heated?
- How long should the experience be?
- How lit up is the room going to be?
- What materials are available, and what is the budget?
- When will we receive the sounds for assignment 2?

## Walking through Transfo

The team showed us around the whole site and told us about its history as a power station, and how they are now planning to make it greener and create a park in the area. They also explained how the evening itself will work: where people will start, what experiences there will be along the way and the approximate path people will take.

We learned that peak hours, when people arrive, are between 18:00 and 19:00, and that the Pompenzaal can get quite full during those hours. Because the live music is at the bar at the end, most visitors will probably walk the route only once. When someone opens the door to the Pompenzaal, there might be music coming from the next room. Our installation is one moment in a longer evening, so it has to work for people arriving in the middle of the flow, and it should fit with what they have just seen.

## What we learned about the Pompenzaal

The Pompenzaal is the room for assignment one, and the visit answered most of our questions about it:

- The room will be heated.
- The acoustics are not great and the room is very echoey. That makes detailed sound difficult, but the echo itself could become part of the work, for example with words that echo.
- We can divide the room however we want: we can work in one big room or divide it with drapes.
- Visitors like simplicity. The room is large, but we don't have to worry about filling up the full space.
- The lighting is also up to us, but usually less is more.
- Referring to the industrial feel and history of the building is a plus, but it doesn't have to be about electricity.

## Theme, budget and materials

We were told that we don't have to be very literal with the theme Breath: the interpretation can be abstract and also very subtle. They said there are parts of the event that don't apply the theme Breath at all, so it is not something we need to worry about.

There is a budget set aside for our group. We send a list of what we need, and the team first checks what is available in Zwevegem. If something is too expensive, they look for alternatives to rent. Building materials are usually available. After our visit, they sent us a PDF with all the tools they have available.

## Practical details

- They expect the production to take up two weeks. During the final week, we will have a room available to work in while we set everything up.
- We can contact the team with any questions, and we can visit the site again whenever we need. The area is always open, but to get inside the building we have to let them know first.
- The sounds that were supposed to be delivered for the second assignment will not arrive soon, so assignment 2 wasn't really an option for us.

## What we take with us

The visit gave us what we needed to make a choice, and we chose assignment one: an interactive installation in the Pompenzaal.

A few things will guide our next steps:

1. **Design for the flow.** Visitors arrive in the middle of a route, right after a performance, and they will only pass by once. The experience has to be clear within seconds.
2. **One big gesture.** Instead of filling the room with many small things, we want one strong idea that fills the space.
3. **Work with the room, not against it.** The echo, the darkness and the industrial character are part of the experience.
4. **Plan early.** Projectors, lights and materials need to be requested weeks ahead, so our technical plan has to be ready soon.
